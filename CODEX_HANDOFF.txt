1. WHAT THE PROJECT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) sequences every spot on a fixed capture grid, so
cost scales with section area rather than biological content. Active learning
(AL) is the obvious way to spend a smaller budget well. This project tests —
rather than assumes — whether that works.

Design: a retrospective pool-based benchmark. Fully profiled public cohorts
have their candidate expression vectors masked; a strategy selects spots over
six acquisition rounds inside one continuous training run; a fixed
morphology-to-expression predictor trains on whatever was acquired; predictions
are scored on held-out patients. Within a budget, every arm shares the initial
pool, the acquisition schedule, the predictor, the optimizer, and the epoch
count — so any difference is attributable to which spots were chosen.

  Strategies (5): random (baseline), mc_dropout, tod (temporal output
                  discrepancy), coreset, typiclust (TypiClust-inspired)
  Budgets (4):    beta = 0.05, 0.10, 0.30, 0.50 of the fold-wide training pool
                  plus a separate beta = 1.0 full-label reference
  Folds (4):      patient-level cross-validation
  Predictor:      ST-Net-style — DenseNet-121 (torchvision IMAGENET1K_V1) ->
                  GAP -> 1024 linear -> ReLU -> dropout -> 300 outputs, MSE
  Targets:        first 300 alphabetically ordered genes, log(1 + raw count),
                  NO library-size normalization (this matters — see 5(f))

FINDING 1 — On the two cancer cohorts, active selection LOSES at small budgets.
  HER2, pooled over the four active strategies, mean paired difference from
  Random: -0.0176 at 5%, -0.0117 at 10%, +0.0055 at 30%, +0.0057 at 50%.
  Three of four strategies fall below Random at 5% on both cohorts; all four
  fall below at 10%. CoreSet is worst at 5% (-0.0412 on HER2, 0/4 folds).

FINDING 2 — On kidney the result INVERTS, and it replicates across seeds.
  Strategy minus Random, mean over 4 folds, per seed (42 / 43 / 44), with
  pooled fold-seed wins out of 12:

    coreset     5%   +0.0258 +0.0196 +0.0237   12/12
    coreset    10%   +0.0116 +0.0170 +0.0184   12/12
    coreset    30%   +0.0166 +0.0119 +0.0151   11/12
    coreset    50%   +0.0129 +0.0156 +0.0103   12/12
    mc_dropout  5%   +0.0226 +0.0200 +0.0177   12/12
    mc_dropout 10%   +0.0128 +0.0174 +0.0190   12/12
    mc_dropout 30%   +0.0162 +0.0102 +0.0129   12/12
    mc_dropout 50%   +0.0133 +0.0143 +0.0069   11/12
    tod         all four budgets positive on all three seeds, 8-12/12
    typiclust   positive except the 10% cell (-0.0034/+0.0008/-0.0019, 4/12)

  CoreSet and MC-dropout each win 47 of 48 fold-seed comparisons. The advantage
  does NOT decay with budget (mean over strategies: +0.0144, +0.0100, +0.0103,
  +0.0116 at 5/10/30/50%), which argues against a "small-budget variance"
  explanation and for a structural one.

FINDING 3 — Mechanism evidence, from the persisted selection indices.
  Per-patient coverage ratio (selected share / pool share), kidney, 16 seed
  cells at the small budgets:

    random     5%   CV 0.153   corr(patient size) -0.03
    coreset    5%   CV 0.423   corr(patient size) -0.55
    mc_dropout 5%   CV 0.409   corr(patient size) -0.07

  CoreSet systematically redistributes budget from large patients to small ones
  (kidney patients span 317-4,166 spots, a 13x range, and each fold holds out
  5-6 unseen patients). MC-dropout deviates from proportional sampling just as
  much but NOT along patient size — so there are at least two distinct
  mechanisms, not one. No patient receives zero coverage under any strategy.
  TREAT THIS AS HYPOTHESIS-SUPPORTED, NOT ESTABLISHED: it rests on 16 cells and
  the 30%/50% selection indices have not been analysed yet (see 5(a)).

FINDING 4 — The evaluation measure changes the ranking, but that does not
  replicate. On HER2, CoreSet and MC-dropout beat Random on expression-cluster
  agreement (ARI) at 5% and 10% on all four folds — precisely where they lose
  most on correlation. On cSCC every active strategy is at or below Random at
  those budgets. Report this as a caution about single-measure benchmarks, not
  as a finding about the strategies.

FINDING 5 — Schedule-matched full-label reference. Both the 0.5 arm and the
  beta=1 arm have an epoch-50 snapshot, so they can be compared at equal
  optimizer exposure with no new runs. At epoch 50: HER2 +0.0058 (3/4 folds),
  cSCC +0.0073 (4/4) in favour of beta=1. This dissolves the apparent anomaly
  that a 50% arm slightly exceeded the full-label run — that was the 50-vs-60
  epoch schedule difference, now demonstrated rather than conjectured.

ALSO ESTABLISHED (smaller, but load-bearing for the papers):
  * TypiClust acquisition-cost measurements were contaminated by machine load.
    Slow timings (1,112-1,584 s/round) all come from 2026-08-09/10; clean ones
    (71-83 s/round at the SAME cluster count k=817) from 2026-08-14. Report the
    clean values. The original decision to skip TypiClust's 50% arm rested on
    the contaminated number.
  * Targets carry a large sequencing-depth component: mean gene-to-library-size
    correlation +0.45 on HER2; projecting depth out leaves 22% of the reported
    PCC. Relative strategy comparisons are unaffected; absolute values are not
    comparable to normalized studies.
  * Patch geometry: patches are stored at exactly 224x224, so the Resize in the
    transform is a no-op; no resampling occurs. On 6 of 36 HER2 slides (one
    patient) patches overlap; 15.5% of HER2 spots have a neighbour closer than
    the patch width.
  * Determinism: torch.manual_seed and np.random.seed are set, but
    cudnn.benchmark = True and deterministic algorithms are NOT enabled, so
    runs are not bit-reproducible even at a fixed seed. This is disclosed in
    the paper and should stay disclosed.
